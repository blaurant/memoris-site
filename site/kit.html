The kit, piece by piece.

Everything here is shared by every page of the site: colours and type, header and footer, tags, threads, fold walls, the keepers and the motion engine. Build pages from these pieces. If a piece is missing, ask for it; do not draw a second version.

A page

Pages sit side by side in site/ and are opened straight from disk. Copy this skeleton. The mark

The logo is the keeper's head in profile, beside the word Memoris. It lives in one definition, MARK in kit/keepers.js: two drawings in a 64 by 64 box, free for the mark alone and tile for the mark on the violet square of an icon, each a function of its colours { fg, bg, accent, second, bead }, which is how the logo studies hand a mark over. Colour and type

Violet leads: it is the company's colour, and here it is Memoris and everything that is Memoris's (the keeper, its answers, the phone's screen, the buttons, the mark, the focus ring). The line is drawn in a violet-black ink. The world around it stays light and warm: cream paper, the pink of the head, coral for the person's own memory, marigold for emphasis, pistachio for a change of room. Every value lives once, at the top of kit/kit.css. Use the variables in CSS and M.colors['amber-pale'] in a script (any variable name, without the dashes in front); never write a hex value in a page.

Violet--violet and --violet-deep are the app's own primary and dark. --lilac is the app's light wash: Memoris's screen, and a room of its own with --lilac-deep.
Each keeper colourThree steps: the body, -deep for its shadow, -pale for its belly. --glow is the light of a lantern and the yellow of a fin; --leaf is what a keeper other than Memoris wears.
Text on colourInk on every light room. On --violet, --night and ink, text is --card or --paper. Coral and marigold never carry text in their own colour on paper: use --coral-deep for an error.

Type

Every typographic decision is a variable in the same block of kit/kit.css, and the fonts themselves are loaded by its first line. Changing the typeface of the whole site is that one block: the @import, the three families, the weights the new face has, and two size factors if it runs larger or smaller. Pages use the classes below and these variables; they never name a font, a numeric weight or an axis setting.

.label, for small caps labels only

h2.d, a title, with a turn

h3.d, a smaller title

.lede, the paragraph under a page title.

Body text, at 18 to 21 px. .link is the text link. .fine is the small print.

“.said is how a person speaks.”

Families--font-display titles (.d), --font-text reading text, tags and buttons, --font-ui labels, navigation and small print.
Weights--w-display titles, --w-title small titles and the turned word of an h1, --w-strong buttons and questions, --w-voice what a person says, --w-body-mid, --w-body; --w-ui, --w-ui-mid, --w-ui-strong for the label family.
Axesfont-variation-settings:var(--axes-display) for large titles, var(--axes-title) for small ones, var(--axes-text) for text. Two more for particular settings: var(--axes-statement) for a large statement paragraph set in the text face, var(--axes-caps) for a paragraph in capitals. With a face that has no axes they are all set to normal once.
Slant--style-em for the turned word of a title, --style-voice for speech and captions.
Spacing--track-display, --track-title, --track-label, --word-display, --lead-display.
Size--scale-display multiplies every title: anything with the class d is scaled as a whole (with zoom), so a page's own title sizes follow with nothing to write. Put d on the title itself, never on a block that also holds a drawing. --scale-text multiplies running text: the body size and everything in em under it, ledes, captions, questions. Where a page sets its own size on running text, or on the text of a field, it multiplies it: font-size:calc(... * var(--scale-text)).
Labels--case-label (uppercase or none), --caps-label (font-variant-caps), --style-label and --scale-label act on .label, .who and a flag. A page that makes or resizes its own small caps writes text-transform:var(--case-label), font-variant-caps:var(--caps-label), font-style:var(--style-label) and multiplies its size by var(--scale-label).
Interface--scale-ui multiplies the small interface text set in the label family: navigation, small print, footer, field messages. A page that sets its own size on one of these multiplies it: font-size:calc(... * var(--scale-ui)). Buttons, the scroll-up tag and other controls keep their size whatever the face: a long button already fills a phone's width.
Title leading--lead-plus is added to every title line-height written as a number: line-height:calc(.97 + var(--lead-plus)). A plain .d keeps --lead-display.
The turned word--em-color colours the em of a title (currentColor today; a face with no italic sends violet). --axes-em and --axes-voice are not set today: a face whose italic is a slant axis sets them, and they reach the em of a title and .said, .figcap. A page with its own italic voice writes font-variation-settings:var(--axes-voice,inherit) beside font-style:var(--style-voice).
No faked stylesfont-synthesis:none on the page: a weight or an italic the face does not have is never imitated, so use only the weight variables.
The offset shadowtext-shadow:var(--title-shadow) is the marigold offset under a page title; --title-shadow-warm is the same offset in paper, for a title on the marigold room. --title-shadow-fine is a slightly shorter offset for very large titles, --title-shadow-bold a slightly longer one for small display text. --offset is the geometry alone, to pair with another colour. A face that drops the shadow sets all four to none.

Two units drive first screens. --u follows the smaller of window width and height and sizes drawings. --t sizes type and grows up to 1.24 times --u on wide, short windows so the screen stays full. Both stop at --grow, the size they reach when the window is as wide as the column, and section titles stop there too; the home page, which runs edge to edge, sets --grow higher. On a narrow or upright window the first screen stacks, and its title grows from 42 px at 600 px of window to 90 px.

Space

One scale, defined once in the same block of kit/kit.css: --s-1 to --s-10 are 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 and 128 px. Inside the header, a first screen (.opener, or the section a page marks data-opener) and anything given the class frame, the same names grow with --t on large windows, so a frame keeps its proportions. A page writes its gaps with these names and with the roles below, so the same relationship has the same value on every page.

--gap-flagA flag to its title: 24 px.
--gap-ledeA page title to its lede, and a closing title to its line: 24 px on a phone, 32 px from a tablet up.
--gap-copyA section title to its paragraph: 24 px.
--gap-ctaText to its button: 32 px.
--gap-noteA button to its small print: 12 px. A second line of small print follows 8 px lower.
--gap-figureA section's title block to the drawing or thread under it: 64 to 128 px.
--gap-afterA drawing to the text that follows it: 32 to 64 px.
--perchThe air between a text and the top of a keeper perched on the thread under it: 48 to 96 px.
--startWhere the first line of a first screen sits once the page is stacked: 32 px under the header on a phone, up to 64 px on a tablet.
--room, --room-endThe padding above and below a room, and under the last room of a page.
--gutterThe side edge of the page. The header, first screens, .wrap and the footer all sit on it: 20 to 84 px on a narrow or upright window, the frame's own inset on a wide one. From 1553 px of window .wrap stops growing and centres a --column of 1320 px. --edge is where that column starts, for something pinned to it.
--foldHow far a fold rises above its room. A room that ends on text leaves calc(var(--s-8) + var(--fold)) under its last line.
--target44 px, the smallest height of anything a finger presses.

Keepers

The keepers are seahorses because the part of the brain that makes new memories is the hippocampus, Greek for seahorse. One hand draws them: compose from this library and ask for a new pose when you need one. Never draw your own.

MemorisViolet, always with the satchel: color:violet;prop:satchel as a free keeper, data-scene="phone" or M.phone() on its phone in a hand (under Scenes). Three rules place it. It is never drawn inside a head or a brain shape, anywhere. On the home page it is only ever on its phone. On every other page it is a free keeper by default; the phone is used sparingly there, where a visitor would really have their phone in hand.

Someone closeRose: family, a loved one, the family caregiver.
A professionalAmber. With the stethoscope it is clinical (clinician, nurse) on every page: color:amber;prop:stethoscope. A visiting aide who is not clinical wears the scarf.

Scenes

When several keepers share one drawing, write a scene: a function that returns SVG, registered by name before the page finishes loading. The kit mounts it, gives its line the cel boil and animates it. Everything inside <g class="boil"> is redrawn in three tracings; anything outside stays still, like a painted background.

<svg data-scene="shelf" viewBox="0 0 560 300" role="img" aria-label="..."></svg>

const M = window.Memoris;
M.scenes.shelf = () => {
  const thread = [0, 200, 280, 236, 560, 200];              x1 y1, sag point, x2 y2
  const reader = M.keeper({ pose: 'read', color: 'coral', arm: 'hold', hold: 'book', ink: 3 });
  const [x, y] = M.threadPoint(thread, 0.3);                a point 30% along the thread
  return '<g class="boil">' + M.strand(thread) +
    M.hung(thread, 0.62, { mark: 'key', length: 22, w: 40, h: 52 }) +
    M.hung(thread, 0.8, { mark: 'photo', fill: M.colors['amber-pale'] }) +
    M.seat(reader, x, y, 1, { flip: true, motion: M.sway(0.2) }) + '</g>';
};
M.strand(a) + M.knot(a, 0.3) + M.strand(b, { until: 0.4, frayed: true }) + M.strand(b, { from: 0.56, frayed: 'start' })
M.keeper(options)Same options as data-keeper, as an object. ink is the outline weight: raise it when you seat a keeper small (about 2.8 divided by the scale).
M.seat(k, x, y, scale, {flip, by, motion})Places a keeper with its holding point at x, y. motion is M.sway(phase, degrees) for one on a thread or M.bob(phase, pixels) for one swimming.
M.colorsEvery colour variable of kit.css, read once: M.colors.violet, M.colors['amber-pale']. M.ink, M.card and M.paper are the three used most. M.memoris is the name of Memoris's keeper colour.
M.strand(thread, {color, width, dotted})Draws the thread. M.hung(thread, t, tag) hangs a small tag or {lantern:true} at t along it.
M.strand(thread, {from, until, frayed})Only part of the thread, from and until being 0 to 1 along it. frayed:true frays the far end, frayed:'start' the near one: a thread that broke, or one that does not reach.
M.drawnPath(d), M.drawn(points)For a thread you draw yourself in page coordinates, outside any scene (a rope that follows scroll, a forked string): returns the same path with a hand's slight waver. The waver rides along the line, so it does not shimmer when the line moves, as long as you keep the number of points the same. Stroke it in ink at 2.6 px with round caps.
M.knot(thread, t, {size})A knot tied in the thread at t, with its two ends: a thread that broke and was tied back.
k.anchorsPoints on a keeper in its own coordinates, for leader lines and for placing things: curl head base tip hand eye chest fin, satchel when it is worn, handUp on pose:hoist, pencil and pencilUp on pose:draw. Seated with M.seat(k, x, y, scale) by its curl, a point lands at x + (k.anchors.eye[0] - k.anchors.curl[0]) * scale, and the same for y. With flip, negate the x difference.
M.miniTag, M.lanternThe small tag and the lantern on their own.
M.voice({mouth: [x, y]})Three voice arcs leaving a point toward the left, one after another: someone speaking. It returns its own boiling group; wrap it in a scale(-1 1) to speak toward the right.
CyclesA group with class="cycle" data-loop="30" data-every="4" shows its children one after another, in frames at 12 a second. The voice arcs and the marks above a head use it.

Motion

Two cadences, and they never mix. Drawings move on twos at 12 frames a second, with a boiling line: that is the cartoon. Type, tags, layout and anything tied to scroll move smoothly. If text ever steps, the page reads as lagging.

Cel, automaticLine boil, blinking, fins, hops on hover, eyes on the pointer, tags popping onto threads, M.sway, M.bob, cycles. You get all of it by using keepers and scenes.
.rvFades and settles an element when it enters the window. Stagger with style="--wait:.1s". Put data-wait-fonts on a first screen so its text waits for the fonts.
TagsEvery .hang sways from scroll speed and comes to rest. Nothing to do.
data-driftAny element drifts slightly against the scroll; the value scales it (data-drift=".7").
M.ready(fn)Runs once the kit has drawn everything. Query scene contents from here.
M.settle(fn)Runs at start, on resize and when fonts land. Measure layout here.
M.follow(fn)For anything tied to scroll. Called every frame while the page moves with {y, height, ease}. Move your value toward its target by ease and return true until it arrives. Never set a scroll-driven value straight from the scroll position: wheel steps would show.
M.act(svg, name)A pose drawn with two arm positions switches between them: pose:hoist (hauling a string up) and pose:draw (the two ends of a pencil stroke) each have down and up. Each call is one cel step, so call it at intervals as the page moves (every 60 px of scroll, for instance), never every frame. On the hoist, the string in its fin starts at k.anchors.hand in both positions. On the draw, the tool's point is at k.anchors.pencil and k.anchors.pencilUp, about 36 units apart: put the line being drawn between those two. The eye follows the tool from one position to the other. hold:brush swaps the pencil for a brush.
M.drawOn(svg, t)Draws every line inside an element stroke by stroke, t going from 0 to 1, in the order the lines were made. Made for stage:rough, and it works on any drawing that is only lines. Drive it from M.follow; it is reversible.
M.nod(svg)Makes the keepers in that drawing hop, to answer a click.
Two trapsDo not set overscroll-behavior on a sticky or scrolling element inside the page: WebKit then stops passing wheel scroll to the page over it. Do not size a .held with CSS scale: use data-size or data-fit.
Reduced motionThe kit stops the cel clock, the sway and every follower, and shows reveals at once. Write your settle so the page is complete without motion: check M.still.matches and set the final state.
M.ready(() => {
  const el = document.querySelector('.thing');
  let shown = 0, top = 0;
  M.settle(() => { top = el.getBoundingClientRect().top + scrollY; if (M.still.matches) shown = 1; render(); });
  M.follow(({ y, height, ease }) => {
    const goal = Math.min(Math.max((y + height - top) / height, 0), 1);
    if (Math.abs(goal - shown) < 0.0004) return false;
    shown += (goal - shown) * ease;
    render();
    return true;
  });
});

Rules

  • One hand for the characters. Compose from the library; ask for a pose, a prop or a mark that is missing.
  • Violet with the satchel is Memoris, on every page. Violet is Memoris's alone: no other keeper, prop or drawing takes it.
  • What a person says or takes is a tag on a thread. Do not invent cards, bubbles or boxes.
  • Backgrounds are flat paint with no outline. Only characters and what they touch carry the ink line.
  • Cel cadence for drawings, smooth for everything else.
  • No "Fig." and no numbers on captions.
  • Not yet is dashed and unpainted: tag--planned, thread--dotted, planned:true, and stage:ink for a keeper.
  • Casting holds on every page: violet is Memoris, coral the person's memory, rose someone close, amber a professional. Colour alone tells them apart.
  • Memoris is never drawn inside a head or a brain shape, anywhere. On the home page it is only ever on its phone (M.phone). On the other pages it is a free keeper by default, and the phone is used sparingly, where a visitor would really have their phone in hand.
  • Colours and type come from the variables of kit.css. No hex value, font name, numeric weight or axis setting in a page.
  • No em dashes, anywhere.
  • Nothing goes into kit/ from a page. If a piece should be shared, ask.
